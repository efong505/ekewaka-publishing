## Purpose and boundary

This artifact synthesizes accepted Phase 1 evidence into Phase 2 recommendations. It is not an execution authorization. It does not constitute legal, trademark, tax, accounting, contract, vendor, pricing, royalty, acquisition, publication, marketing, or deployment approval.

## 1. Recommended legal-entity posture

**Recommendation:** retain **Ekewaka Publishing LLC** as the preferred New Mexico legal-entity concept for Human Owner consideration.

Rationale:
- Phase 1 supports a New Mexico LLC as the most coherent initial structure for an owner-controlled independent publishing company.
- The structure can support publisher operations, contracts, vendor relationships, title economics, and later separated business lines without requiring Phase 2 to decide tax elections.
- The recommendation remains conditional on qualified New Mexico business-attorney and CPA/tax review before filing or tax-election decisions.

**Not established:** legal sufficiency, filing readiness, tax election, registered-agent posture, local licensing completeness, or final entity approval.

## 2. Recommended working brand / name posture

**Recommendation:** retain **Ekewaka Publishing** as the preferred working public-facing brand and **Ekewaka Publishing LLC** as the preferred legal-entity concept.

**Secondary candidate:** retain **Ekewaka Press** only as a backup imprint/name candidate if later catalog or brand architecture creates a clear reason.

The Phase 1 registry evidence found no blocking exact-result defect in the reviewed searches, but that does not equal legal clearance. Final adoption must remain subject to:
- comprehensive federal/state/common-law trademark clearance;
- current registry rechecks immediately before material reliance or filing;
- live domain/social availability checks before acquisition or public launch;
- Human Owner approval.

## 3. Proposed publishing mission

**Proposed mission for Human Owner consideration:**

> Ekewaka Publishing exists to publish carefully selected books with clear human accountability, durable editorial standards, transparent author relationships, and disciplined business practices. It begins with Edward Fong's catalog and may later publish selected outside authors whose work fits the company's editorial, ethical, and commercial standards, while keeping publisher-funded trade publishing clearly separate from any author-paid professional services.

This mission intentionally avoids promising sales, bestseller outcomes, bookstore placement, unrestricted submissions, or immediate outside-author acquisition.

## 4. Proposed positioning

Ekewaka should position itself as a **selective independent publisher with strong governance, transparent lane separation, human editorial accountability, and technology-enabled operations**.

Recommended positioning principles:
1. selective rather than volume-driven;
2. evidence-based acquisition and title economics;
3. publisher-funded risk in Lane A;
4. transparent, separately contracted fee-for-service work in Lane C if later launched;
5. no disguised pay-to-publish treatment inside Lane A;
6. explicit human review and accountability for AI-assisted workflows;
7. durable title, rights, ISBN, metadata, accounting, and audit records;
8. conservative growth paced by operational capacity.

## 5. Proposed initial catalog scope

**Founding scope:** Edward Fong's existing and future titles are the initial catalog foundation, subject to title-by-title ISBN/provenance and migration decisions in the later governed catalog-migration phase.

**Outside-author scope:** do not open general submissions in Phase 2. Preserve the previously preferred closed/solicited model for future governed development. Any outside-author title must later pass acquisition, rights, financial, production, and Human Owner approval gates.

**Genre/category posture:** Phase 2 should not hard-code a narrow genre list without additional catalog/market evidence. The initial architecture should instead require every prospective title to fit the approved mission, catalog strategy, editorial capacity, audience logic, and title-level economics.

## 6. Recommended three-lane architecture

### Lane A — Ekewaka Select / Trade Publishing

**Recommended status:** primary publishing model for future development.

Core boundary:
- publisher-funded publication risk;
- selective acquisition;
- author does not pay a publication fee;
- specific publishing rights licensed by contract;
- Ekewaka-controlled publisher ISBN for Ekewaka-published editions unless a governed exception applies;
- title-level economics required before any offer;
- Human Owner approval required for every outside-author acquisition/offer.

### Lane B — Ekewaka Partner / Hybrid / Co-Investment

**Recommended status:** research-only / dormant.

No marketing, pilot, client intake, author contribution, or launch is recommended or authorized. Any future reconsideration requires a distinct governed proposal demonstrating professional-standard alignment, transparent economics, real publisher contribution, attorney/CPA review, and explicit Human Owner approval.

### Lane C — Ekewaka Author Services

**Recommended status:** future separate business line, not launched.

If later developed:
- customer remains publisher wherever practicable;
- customer-controlled ISBNs/accounts/revenue routing are preferred where appropriate;
- services use defined scopes, limits, deliverables, change-order rules, and disclaimers;
- no implied Lane A acquisition;
- no sales, ranking, review, bestseller, bookstore, library, or media-outcome guarantees;
- pricing must follow bottom-up labor/vendor costing, current market validation, legal/tax review, and Human Owner approval.

## 7. Separation controls

Lane separation should be enforced through separate:
- contracts and disclosures;
- accounting/revenue classification;
- ISBN/publisher-of-record treatment;
- author/customer onboarding;
- marketing language and website navigation;
- approval workflows;
- financial models;
- rights records;
- operating procedures.

A person purchasing Lane C services must not be represented as having been acquired by Lane A.

## 8. Printing/distribution posture for architecture purposes

Phase 2 should retain a multi-channel architecture concept:
- KDP for Amazon-native POD;
- Ingram for potential bookstore/library/trade reach;
- direct sales as a separately modeled channel;
- Lulu and short-run/offset vendors as decision candidates where economics/specifications justify them.

This is architecture only. No vendor is selected. Live landed-cost, quote, returns, freight, storage, fulfillment, 3PL, tax, and title-specific evidence remains required before implementation decisions.

## 9. ISBN / metadata / rights posture

Recommended architecture:
- publisher-controlled ISBNs for future Lane A editions;
- central ISBN/edition ledger;
- canonical metadata registry plus channel-observed state;
- rights registry linked to title, contract, edition, and revenue records;
- explicit Lane A/B/C publisher-of-record and rights boundaries;
- identifier decision record for every new ISBN assignment or retained-ISBN decision.

No ISBN block purchase, assignment, account architecture, final imprint, rights grant, or outside-author rights action is approved here.

## 10. Financial architecture posture

Phase 2 should preserve:
- channel-specific title P&Ls;
- contribution margin and cash timing as acquisition controls;
- downside/stress cases;
- return reserves and inventory exposure as first-class economics;
- Lane B segregation;
- Lane C bottom-up costing before public pricing;
- live rechecks before material commercial decisions.

`VER06-C1` and `VER06-C2` remain active and must be resolved at their applicable material-reliance gates.
