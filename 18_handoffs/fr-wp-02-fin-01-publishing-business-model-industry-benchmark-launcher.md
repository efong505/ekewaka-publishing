**FR-WP-02 — Publishing Business Model & Industry Benchmark**

This is foundational research only.

## Required scope

### A. Industry business-model benchmark
Research current, defensible publishing-business models relevant to Ekewaka Publishing, including:
- traditional/trade publishing;
- independent/small-press publishing;
- hybrid/co-investment publishing;
- paid author-services/self-publishing service companies;
- relevant direct-to-consumer publisher models.

Clearly distinguish who pays, who carries risk, who owns/controls rights, who owns ISBNs, who controls retail/distribution accounts, and how revenue flows.

### B. Lane A — Ekewaka Select / Trade Publishing
Benchmark economics and operating norms relevant to a selective publisher-funded acquisition model, including:
- advances or no-advance structures;
- royalties and royalty bases;
- author copies;
- editorial/production investment;
- print/distribution economics;
- wholesale discount and returns exposure;
- direct-sales economics;
- publisher margin and break-even drivers;
- rights scope and subsidiary-rights economics at a high level;
- practical economics for a small/new press.

Do not design final royalty terms or contracts.

### C. Lane B — Ekewaka Partner / Hybrid / Co-Investment
Research only. Do not authorize launch.

Benchmark transparent, professionally defensible hybrid/co-investment structures and contrast them with vanity/pay-to-publish models. Evaluate:
- author investment models;
- publisher investment/risk sharing;
- preorder or commitment thresholds;
- author-copy commitments;
- revenue and royalty economics;
- rights/control expectations;
- disclosure/transparency standards;
- accreditation or professional standards where relevant;
- consumer-protection/reputational risks.

Identify whether this lane should remain research-only, proceed to design later, or be rejected based on evidence. Do not make the Human Owner decision.

### D. Lane C — Ekewaka Author Services
Benchmark current paid publishing-service models and economics at a business-model level, without final package/pricing design. Research:
- common service categories;
- typical project structures;
- market price ranges where reliable evidence exists;
- labor/vendor intensity;
- gross-margin considerations;
- revision/scope-creep risk;
- customer ownership of rights and publishing accounts;
- refund/guarantee/consumer-protection issues;
- separation from publisher-funded acquisition.

SERV-01 owns later package architecture. FIN-01 should provide the economic benchmark SERV-01 will need.

### E. Competitor / comparator benchmark
Select a defensible comparator set across traditional/small press, hybrid/co-investment, and author-service providers. Include relevant named comparators only where reliable evidence supports the comparison.

For each, capture where reasonably available:
- business model/lane;
- author-paid vs publisher-funded;
- rights posture;
- ISBN/publisher-of-record posture;
- distribution/retail positioning;
- pricing/fees where public;
- royalty/economic structure where public;
- notable strengths/risks;
- evidence quality and limitations.

Do not infer confidential economics.

### F. Ekewaka preliminary business-model implications
Using evidence, assess the initial three-lane architecture:
1. Ekewaka Select / Trade Publishing;
2. Ekewaka Partner / Hybrid / Co-Investment;
3. Ekewaka Author Services.

Identify:
- which assumptions are supported;
- which require modification;
- which remain unresolved;
- what must be financially modeled in FR-WP-06;
- what must go to attorney/CPA/professional review;
- what remains a Human Owner decision.

Do not convert recommendations into approvals.
