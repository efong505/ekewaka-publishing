## Required work-package architecture

The preparation package must define:

### 1. Scope and exclusions
Specify exactly what P3-WP-01 will and will not do.

P3-WP-01 should design operating-model workflows, controls, artifacts, roles, handoffs, registers, readiness criteria, and decision gates.

It must not itself:
- acquire an outside author;
- make or authorize an offer;
- execute a publishing or services agreement;
- set final royalty, advance, pricing, refund, reserve, or margin terms;
- purchase or assign ISBNs;
- select or commit a vendor/printer/distributor;
- place print orders;
- launch Lane B or Lane C;
- form the LLC;
- make tax elections;
- open bank/accounts;
- conduct paid marketing;
- publish an outside-author title;
- deploy production payment/royalty/accounting systems;
- commit material funds.

### 2. Specialist roles
Define the bounded roles and handoffs for at minimum:
- PM-01 — orchestration/integration;
- ACQ-01 — acquisitions / author relations;
- PROD-01 — editorial / production / quality;
- RIGHTS-01 — ISBN / metadata / rights;
- DIST-01 — printing / distribution / fulfillment architecture;
- ROY-01 — accounting / royalty operations;
- FIN-01 — title/company economics where needed;
- LEGAL-01 / CONTRACT-01 — legal-research / contract-framework boundaries;
- VER-01 — independent verification.

Do not allow a specialist to self-verify.

### 3. Lane boundaries
Carry forward:
- Lane A as the core publisher-funded trade-publishing model;
- Lane B as dormant / research-only;
- Lane C as a separately governed optional demand-led services lane.

P3-WP-01 must not blur:
- acquisitions versus services;
- author versus customer;
- publishing rights versus service permissions;
- publisher ISBN/account control versus customer-controlled service-lane accounts;
- book-sale revenue versus service revenue.

### 4. Core operating workflows
The work package definition should require Phase 3 to design, at minimum:

#### Acquisition / author-relations workflow
- title/candidate intake architecture;
- evaluation criteria;
- acquisition memo;
- rights/economics/readiness gates;
- Human Owner offer approval;
- author onboarding;
- relationship lifecycle;
- rejection/hold/decline states;
- no general submission launch unless separately authorized.

#### Editorial / production workflow
- developmental/copy/proof stages;
- production specifications;
- covers/interiors/ebook;
- source-file controls;
- proofs and corrections;
- accessibility/complex-format considerations;
- release-readiness and quality-control checklist;
- revision/change control.

#### ISBN / metadata / rights workflow
- ISBN/edition decision record;
- canonical metadata;
- rights registry;
- title/format/edition linkage;
- rights-grant/retention tracking;
- permissions and provenance;
- reversion/termination/wind-down controls;
- account ownership/recovery boundaries.

#### Contract-framework workflow
- required agreement types and clauses/categories;
- routing to qualified publishing/IP/business counsel;
- version control and approval;
- signature authority;
- no execution without Human Owner and professional-review gates.

#### Printing / distribution / fulfillment workflow
- KDP/Ingram/direct/Lulu/short-run/offset decision inputs;
- landed-cost refresh requirements;
- returns/discount/reserve controls;
- print-order/procurement approval gates;
- inventory and 3PL decisions;
- channel setup/metadata coordination.

#### Royalty/accounting workflow
- title-level ledger architecture;
- revenue-source mapping;
- royalty basis and statements;
- reserves/returns/accruals;
- reconciliation;
- payment approval;
- audit trail;
- cash timing;
- lane separation;
- no production financial-system deployment in this preparation cycle.

#### Quality / release governance
- pre-publication release gate;
- metadata/rights/file/economics checks;
- legal/professional checks where applicable;
- Human Owner publication approval;
- post-release correction/escalation process.
