# FR-WP-02 — Lane B: Hybrid / Co-Investment Benchmark & Risk Analysis

**Retrieval date:** 2026-09-08  
**Status:** Research only. Lane B is not authorized for launch.

## 1. Definition benchmark

IBPA defines a hybrid publisher as a publisher using an author-subsidized business model while otherwise behaving as a professional publisher. Its current 11-point criteria require a hybrid publisher to:

1. define a mission and vision;
2. vet submissions;
3. commit to truth and transparency;
4. provide a negotiable, understandable contract;
5. publish under its own imprint and ISBNs;
6. publish to professional industry standards;
7. ensure editorial/design/production quality;
8. pursue/manage publishing rights;
9. provide distribution services;
10. demonstrate respectable sales;
11. pay higher-than-standard royalties when royalties are used.

Primary source: https://www.ibpa-online.org/page/hybridpublisher

The defining distinction from Lane C is therefore not merely that money changes hands. In a genuine hybrid relationship, the hybrid company remains a **publisher**, with publishing responsibility and participation in title economics.

## 2. Risk-sharing structures to test later

The launcher authorizes research into several possible co-investment forms. The following are economic structures for later modeling, not endorsed offerings:

| Structure | Author contribution | Publisher contribution/risk | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed production contribution | Predetermined cash contribution | Publisher manages/funds remaining approved work | Can resemble vanity publishing if publisher contribution/value is weak |
| Preorder threshold | Author/market generates committed demand before full production | Publisher invests after threshold | Refunds, timing, fulfillment and disclosure complexity |
| Guaranteed author-copy commitment | Author commits to defined copy quantity | Publisher invests in production/distribution | Authors Guild flags mandatory copy purchases as problematic in hybrid contracts; high reputational risk |
| Shared production budget | Contracted percentage/cost allocation | Shared cash exposure | Requires transparent budgets and change controls |
| Limited partnership/pilot | Bounded author cohort meeting strict selection criteria | Publisher assumes defined catalog/distribution duties | Operational complexity and brand confusion |

Because the Authors Guild’s July 2026 hybrid guidance specifically states that an author should never have to buy copies to induce a publisher to sign an agreement, FIN-01 does **not** recommend a mandatory author-copy purchase as a preferred Lane B mechanism. Source: https://authorsguild.org/resource/what-authors-should-know-about-hybrid-publishing-contracts/

## 3. Current comparator — She Writes Press

She Writes Press publicly states that its single publishing package costs **$12,000 as of January 2026**. Publicly listed inclusions include custom cover/interior design, ebook conversion/distribution, proofreading, copyright filing/LCCN support, warehousing, fulfillment, metadata management, ongoing project management and trade distribution through Simon & Schuster.

Source: https://shewritespress.com/our-package/

Implications:

- a credible hybrid can involve a materially larger author investment than basic author services;
- the fee supports an ongoing publisher infrastructure rather than only files/design;
- trade distribution and publisher lifecycle management are material differentiators;
- the author contribution alone does not prove economic value; sales and author ROI remain title-specific.

## 4. Current comparator — Greenleaf Book Group

Greenleaf describes itself as a full-service hybrid publisher and distributor. It states that authors retain rights and creative control, but must bring an investment mindset because the author funds the process. Greenleaf states that it reviews submissions, that its costs vary by project and are provided after acceptance, and that its distribution reaches major retailers/wholesalers.

Sources:

- https://greenleafbookgroup.com/
- https://greenleafbookgroup.com/about/faq
- https://greenleafbookgroup.com/learning-center/book-creation/what-makes-a-greenleaf-author

Greenleaf does not publish a standard public fee in the evidence reviewed. FIN-01 therefore does not infer one.

## 5. Consumer-protection and reputational evidence

### Authors Guild

Current 2026 guidance emphasizes:

- no extraordinary sales/bestseller guarantees;
- marketing deliverables must be specific;
- authors should scrutinize one-sided clauses;
- scams and impersonation are increasing;
- traditional publishers do not charge up front; hybrid claims require diligence.

Sources:

- https://authorsguild.org/resource/what-authors-should-know-about-hybrid-publishing-contracts/
- https://authorsguild.org/resource/avoiding-publishing-scams/

### Society of Authors / Writers’ Guild of Great Britain

Their paid-for publishing investigation found widespread complaints regarding aggressive sales, unclear contracts and poor value. Their published open-letter principles call for explicit disclosure of business model, itemized costs, realistic return information, professional production capacity and specific marketing commitments.

Source: https://societyofauthors.org/end-bad-practice-in-hybrid-paid-for-publishing-open-letter/

This is UK industry/consumer-practice evidence, not a statement of U.S. law. It nevertheless identifies material reputational patterns that Ekewaka should avoid.

### Federal consumer-protection principle

The FTC’s OMICS enforcement concerned academic publishing rather than trade books, so it is not a direct comparator. It is nevertheless authoritative evidence that deceptive publishing claims and hidden fees can support serious consumer-protection enforcement. Ekewaka should not generalize the case beyond its facts, but the principle reinforces transparent representations and fee disclosure.

Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/152-3113-omics-group-inc

## 6. Minimum economic conditions if Lane B is ever designed

Before Lane B could responsibly move beyond research, the following would need to be validated:

- measurable publisher investment or at-risk contribution;
- itemized author contribution and what it funds;
- no hidden mandatory upsells;
- author economics demonstrably superior to Lane A benchmark;
- channel-specific net-revenue allocation;
- return reserves and inventory treatment;
- clear publisher ISBN/imprint treatment;
- clear rights grant and reversion;
- specific marketing/sales commitments without guarantees;
- transparent author-copy pricing;
- title-level budget and break-even analysis;
- cancellation/refund/change-order rules;
- independent publishing-counsel review;
- CPA/accounting treatment for author contributions, royalties, refunds and returns;
- sufficient operations to satisfy IBPA-level production/distribution expectations;
- Human Owner approval.

## 7. Lane B economic/reputation risk register

| Risk | Severity | Why material | Preliminary control |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Vanity-model perception | Critical | Author payment can overwhelm publisher-sales identity | Strict criteria, disclosure, real vetting, publisher investment/responsibility |
| Lane A/Lane B confusion | Critical | Authors may perceive paid route as purchase of acquisition | Separate brand language, contracts, accounting and eligibility |
| Low author ROI | High | High upfront investment can exceed likely royalties | Scenario model, no ROI guarantee, realistic sales sensitivity |
| Mandatory copy pressure | High | Current Authors Guild guidance flags it | Avoid as acceptance condition; model optional bulk/preorders separately |
| Vague marketing | Critical | Major complaint/scam vector | Itemized deliverables, budget, exclusions |
| Rights overreach | Critical | Author invested yet could lose broad rights/control | Narrow rights, reversion, counsel review |
| Weak distribution | Critical | Hybrid credibility depends on real distribution/sales | FR-WP-03 validation and measurable channel capability |
| Cross-subsidy opacity | High | Fees could mask unsustainable publisher economics | Lane-specific title P&L and revenue recognition |
| Refund/cancellation disputes | High | High author payment increases consumer exposure | Written milestone/cancellation/refund architecture |

## 8. FIN-01 recommendation

**Keep Lane B research-only.**

Evidence supports hybrid publishing as a legitimate model only when it clears a much higher bar than “author pays part of the cost.” Ekewaka currently has no accepted FR-WP-06 model, no approved hybrid contract architecture, no accepted distribution operating model, and no demonstrated hybrid sales record. Advancing Lane B to launch design now would create disproportionate RISK-002/RISK-009 exposure.

A later PM may route a bounded Lane B design study after FR-WP-03, FR-WP-05 and FR-WP-06 mature. Any launch remains a Human Owner gate.
