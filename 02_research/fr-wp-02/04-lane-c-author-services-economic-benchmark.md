# FR-WP-02 — Lane C: Author-Services Economic Benchmark

**Retrieval date:** 2026-09-08  
**Purpose:** Business-model economics only. SERV-01 owns later package architecture and FIN-01 must validate economics before any pricing is accepted.

## 1. Economic identity

Lane C is a fee-for-service business. The author/customer pays for clearly defined professional deliverables and generally remains the publisher of record. The cleanest default architecture is:

- customer retains copyright and publishing rights;
- customer controls their ISBN/publishing identity;
- customer controls KDP/Ingram/retail accounts wherever practicable;
- Ekewaka receives only narrowly necessary service licenses;
- Ekewaka earns service fees rather than a publisher royalty from the customer's book sales;
- payment for services never implies acquisition into Lane A.

## 2. Public market benchmarks

### Destiny House Publishing

Destiny House identifies itself as a Christian self-publishing/on-demand service company and states that authors retain 100% of publishing rights and set retail price.

Main package page observed 2026-09-08:

- Blue — $799
- Silver — $1,099
- Platinum — $1,399
- Diamond — $1,900

Source: https://destinyhousepublishing.com/

Individual product pages retrieved during the same research displayed lower sale/deposit figures for Silver, Platinum and Diamond. This inconsistency means FIN-01 does not treat any Destiny House price as a durable market rate without live recheck. The package architecture remains useful as a scope comparator: cover/interior, editing/proofing, ebook, graphics, copies, website/video/collateral at higher tiers.

Destiny House also publishes author-copy pricing by page range, currently beginning at $6 per copy under 200 pages and scaling upward by page band, plus shipping/tax. Source: https://destinyhousepublishing.com/products/authors-order-books-here

### BookBaby

BookBaby’s current public guidance states:

- a professional fully produced book with editing, custom cover, formatting and meaningful first print run typically falls around **$2,000–$7,000**;
- typical full-service bundles are described around **$1,500–$3,000**;
- cover design starts at $399 in its public pricing discussion;
- price depends heavily on page count, trim, binding, color, paper and quantity.

Sources:

- https://www.bookbaby.com/pricing
- https://www.bookbaby.com/resources/self-publishing-cost

These are BookBaby market statements, not independently audited averages.

### Reedsy marketplace data

Reedsy’s 2026 published marketplace analysis gives stronger task-level cost context:

- professional editing for an 80,000-word manuscript: approximately **$2,160–$5,040** depending on editorial scope;
- professional cover design average: approximately **$880**;
- most cover projects: approximately **$625–$1,250**.

Sources:

- https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/how-to-self-publish-a-book/cost-to-self-publish/
- https://reedsy.com/blog/book-cover-design-cost/

Reedsy is a marketplace/secondary benchmark, not a pricing authority. It is useful for showing that skilled editorial/design labor can exceed the sticker price of low-cost bundled publishing packages.

## 3. Core service-cost drivers

### Editorial

Editorial cost is usually driven by:

- word count;
- editorial level (assessment, developmental, line/copy, proof);
- manuscript condition;
- specialist/technical complexity;
- number of passes;
- turnaround time;
- author revision cycle.

A package that simply says “editing included” without word limits and editorial definition is financially unsafe.

### Design / production

Cost drivers include:

- custom vs template cover;
- stock art/licensing/illustration;
- interior complexity;
- tables/images/footnotes;
- ebook conversion complexity;
- hardcover/large-print/workbook variants;
- proof/revision rounds.

### Web / marketing assets

Websites, trailers, graphics, press kits and event collateral have high scope-creep potential because creative review cycles can be subjective. Any later package should specify page counts, hosting/domain exclusions, revision rounds, source assets, ownership and ongoing-maintenance exclusions.

### Included author copies

Copies are not “free” economically. Every included copy requires print, shipping/handling and sometimes fulfillment labor. SERV-01 should treat included copies as an explicit package cost allowance.

## 4. Recommended internal costing framework for later pricing

For every service item:

`Fully loaded service cost = internal labor hours × internal loaded hourly cost + contractor/vendor cost + direct software/asset cost + QA/rework allowance + admin/payment cost`

For a package:

`Package contribution = package fee − all included service costs − included copies/shipping − payment processing − allocated support/admin`

Then calculate:

`Package gross margin % = package contribution / package fee`

FR-WP-02 does not set a required gross margin. FR-WP-06 should model a minimum acceptable floor and sensitivity to overruns.

## 5. Scope-creep controls required for Lane C

Later package architecture should explicitly define:

- manuscript word/page ceiling;
- exact editing level;
- number of editorial passes;
- number of design concepts;
- number of revision rounds;
- client response deadlines;
- what counts as a change order;
- third-party/vendor allowances;
- included copies and shipping treatment;
- timeline assumptions;
- cancellation/refund rules;
- responsibility for permissions/fact/legal review;
- marketing deliverables and exclusions;
- no sales, review, placement or bestseller guarantees.

Without these controls, low sticker prices can create negative margins and dissatisfaction.

## 6. Account / ISBN / rights posture

Lane C should generally avoid using Ekewaka’s publisher ISBN or publisher retail accounts because doing so blurs the relationship into publishing. The preferable default is:

- customer-owned ISBN if an ISBN is required;
- customer-owned KDP/Ingram accounts;
- Ekewaka may configure metadata/files under customer authorization;
- customer receives editable/source files when contractually included;
- provider access is removable at project completion;
- customer title revenue flows directly to customer rather than through Ekewaka where feasible.

RIGHTS-01 and counsel must later validate exact implementation.

## 7. Refund / consumer-protection risk

High-risk patterns identified in current author-industry guidance include:

- vague promises of marketing;
- implied sales/bookstore/bestseller outcomes;
- undisclosed add-ons;
- unclear cancellation/refund terms;
- provider control of rights/accounts after paid service completion;
- aggressive sales pressure;
- confusing service purchase with selective acquisition.

Ekewaka should design Lane C to sell **defined professional work**, not publishing dreams or outcome guarantees.

## 8. Preliminary Lane C conclusion

**Economically plausible and suitable for later SERV-01 architecture, but margin depends on rigorous scope control.**

Lane C may become a useful service-revenue stream that is less capital-intensive than Lane A. It is not automatically low risk: editorial hours, custom creative work, vendor rework, client revisions, refunds and support can rapidly consume margin. Package prices should therefore be derived from measured internal/vendor cost and capacity, not copied from competitor prices.
