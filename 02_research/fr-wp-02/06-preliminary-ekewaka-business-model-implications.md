# FR-WP-02 — Preliminary Ekewaka Business-Model Implications

**Retrieval date:** 2026-09-08  
**Governance:** Recommendations only. No Human Owner gate is exercised by this document.

## 1. Three-lane architecture disposition

| Lane | Evidence-supported posture | Key condition |
|---|---|---|
| Lane A — Ekewaka Select / Trade | **Proceed to later architecture/modeling** | Must pass title-level FR-WP-06 acquisition economics before any offer |
| Lane B — Ekewaka Partner / Hybrid | **Remain research-only** | Must demonstrate IBPA-level publisher capability, superior author economics, legal/accounting clarity and Human Owner approval |
| Lane C — Ekewaka Author Services | **Proceed to SERV-01 architecture after/with finance inputs** | Must remain visibly separate from acquisition and use rigorous cost/scope controls |

## 2. Lane A implications

### Supported

- Publisher-funded selective acquisition is consistent with standard trade identity.
- Ekewaka-owned publisher ISBNs and publisher-controlled distribution accounts fit the model.
- Author retention of copyright with a defined rights license is consistent with the Authors Guild model framework.
- POD can reduce inventory exposure while maintaining channel access.
- Direct sales should be deliberately developed because contribution can materially exceed wholesale contribution.

### Must be modified/refined

- A blanket $0 advance policy should remain a working concept rather than a permanent rule.
- Royalty rates cannot be chosen before the channel mix and net contribution are modeled.
- “Net receipts” must not become a vague catch-all deduction definition; publishing counsel should review it.
- Return reserves must be modeled for trade channels.

### Required downstream gate

Every proposed outside-author title should later have an acquisition finance memo containing conservative/base/upside units, channel mix, title investment, contribution per channel, break-even, cash need, return sensitivity and downside loss.

## 3. Lane B implications

### Supported

- Hybrid/co-investment exists as a recognized model when it functions as real publishing rather than disguised services.
- IBPA criteria align with Ekewaka’s desire for selective vetting, professional production, publisher ISBNs, distribution and higher author economics.

### Not yet supportable for launch

- Ekewaka has no accepted hybrid operating track record.
- No validated FR-WP-06 model exists.
- No hybrid contract/refund/accounting architecture exists.
- Distribution capability is not yet accepted through FR-WP-03.
- Exact rights/ISBN mechanics await FR-WP-05.
- Consumer/reputation risk is unusually high if paid and publisher-funded pathways are confused.

### Recommended posture

Do not market or sell Lane B. If a later business case remains attractive, PM-01 should authorize a distinct hybrid design work package after foundational finance/distribution/rights work matures.

## 4. Lane C implications

### Supported

- There is a broad paid market for editorial, design, formatting, ebook, website, launch asset and publishing-setup services.
- Author control of rights, ISBN and publishing accounts is the cleanest separation from Lane A.
- Service fees can generate revenue without placing full title-sales risk on Ekewaka.

### Required modification to package thinking

Packages should not be built by copying competitor inclusions and then choosing a market-looking price. They should be built bottom-up from:

- hours by skill level;
- contractor/vendor quotes;
- revision allowance;
- included-copy cost;
- software/assets;
- payment processing;
- project management/admin;
- quality assurance/rework reserve;
- target contribution/margin.

Only then should SERV-01 compare the calculated price floor to market positioning.

## 5. Financial architecture implications

Ekewaka should maintain separate financial dimensions for:

- Lane A publisher revenue;
- Lane B author contributions (only if later approved);
- Lane B book revenue/royalties;
- Lane C service revenue;
- direct retail revenue;
- Amazon/KDP revenue;
- Ingram/wholesale revenue;
- bulk/institutional revenue;
- author-copy revenue;
- refunds/returns;
- author royalties payable;
- vendor/contractor costs.

The master prompt already prohibits intermingling these economic categories in reporting. FR-WP-02 confirms that this is not merely bookkeeping neatness: each lane has a different economic substance.

## 6. Control of ISBNs and accounts

Preliminary design principle:

- **Lane A:** Ekewaka ISBN and publisher-controlled distribution account.
- **Lane B:** if approved, Ekewaka/hybrid imprint ISBN and publisher distribution account, consistent with IBPA criteria.
- **Lane C:** author/customer ISBN and author-controlled distribution accounts wherever technically practical.

RIGHTS-01 owns the final ISBN/metadata/rights architecture.

## 7. Direct-to-consumer strategy implication

Direct sales should be modeled as a strategic channel rather than an incidental convenience. The merchant receives the retail sale but must absorb payment processing, printing/fulfillment, shipping policies, customer support and refund/chargeback risk. The likely higher unit contribution can justify this operational burden for launches, signed copies, bundles, institutional/church sales and events.

MKT-01, DIST-01 and later SYS/WEB work should therefore treat direct commerce as a first-class channel while preserving conservative assumptions.

## 8. Professional-review map

| Issue | Required review |
|---|---|
| Trade and hybrid rights grants, net-receipts definitions, reversion, audit, warranties | Publishing/IP counsel |
| Hybrid fee/refund/cancellation and consumer representations | Publishing/business counsel; consumer-law review as warranted |
| GRT/sales tax, service revenue, royalties, marketplace treatment, multistate sales | CPA / tax professional |
| Royalty accrual, returns reserve, statements, author-copy accounting | CPA/bookkeeper/ROY-01 |
| ISBN/publisher-of-record implications | RIGHTS-01 + counsel where contractual |
| Distribution discount/returns policy | DIST-01 + FIN-01 model validation |

## 9. Human Owner decisions preserved

The Human Owner still must approve, at minimum, any final:

- business model adoption at Phase 2 gate;
- hybrid launch;
- author-services public launch;
- package price publication;
- contractual royalty structure;
- outside-author acquisition;
- material vendor agreement;
- ISBN purchase;
- public launch or paid marketing.

FR-WP-02 makes none of those decisions.
