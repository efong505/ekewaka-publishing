## Executive disposition

The preferred concept **Ekewaka Publishing LLC** remains a reasonable entity/name candidate for further review, but neither the entity structure nor any Ekewaka mark is legally cleared. New Mexico LLC formation is available through the Secretary of State online filing portal; current evidence supports a $50 domestic LLC articles-of-organization filing fee, a New Mexico registered office/registered agent requirement, and articles containing the statutory information described in the LLC Act. Albuquerque requires a City Business License for most businesses operating a place of business within city limits, including home-based businesses, with zoning review; the current City FAQ states a $36.75 annual fee effective July 1, 2026.

New Mexico Taxation and Revenue states that anyone engaging in business in New Mexico must register and that GRT generally reaches receipts from selling property and performing services in New Mexico. Marketplace-provider/seller rules can create deductions or shifted collection responsibilities, but exact treatment of Amazon/KDP, Ingram, direct book sales, author-service revenue, royalties, and multi-state sourcing is fact-dependent and should be reviewed with a New Mexico CPA/tax adviser before launch.

Federal default classification depends on ownership and elections. A one-member domestic LLC is generally disregarded for federal income-tax purposes unless it elects corporate treatment; multi-member domestic LLCs are generally partnerships unless they elect otherwise. EIN need/use is fact-dependent, although the IRS advises forming the legal entity before applying for an EIN. The 2026 FinCEN final BOI rule exempts U.S.-created companies from BOI reporting; an older New Mexico SOS CTA page is therefore stale and must not be relied upon.

Preliminary web/common-law searches did not surface an obvious publishing house using the exact strings **Ekewaka Publishing**, **Ekewaka Publishing LLC**, or **Ekewaka Press**. That is not trademark clearance. The word/name **Ekewaka** is used by third parties in unrelated contexts, including as a personal/pen name and in books/media. A live USPTO attorney-grade clearance search should include exact and confusingly similar marks, phonetic variants, translations/transliterations as relevant, related publishing/media/education goods and services, and state/common-law uses.

## Confirmed facts

- New Mexico SOS routes current business filings through its online Enterprise portal.
- The New Mexico LLC Act is identified by SOS as NMSA 1978 §§ 53-19-1 through 53-19-74.
- New Mexico law requires an LLC registered office in New Mexico and a registered agent meeting statutory criteria.
- Current SOS-filed domestic LLC evidence states a **$50 non-refundable Articles of Organization fee**.
- Albuquerque requires a Business License for places of business within municipal boundaries; zoning review applies to home-based locations. Current City FAQ states **$36.75 annually effective 2026-07-01**.
- New Mexico TRD says anyone engaging in business in New Mexico must register; GRT includes receipts from selling property in New Mexico and performing services in New Mexico, subject to statutory exemptions/deductions and sourcing rules.
- IRS default classification: one-member domestic LLC generally disregarded; two-or-more-member domestic LLC generally partnership, unless a corporate election is made.
- IRS information-return rules include Form 1099-MISC reporting for copyright-type royalties at the applicable threshold and Form 1099-NEC for reportable nonemployee compensation; current 2026 instructions reflect changed thresholds for some NEC categories, so annual instructions must be rechecked each year.
- FinCEN's August 2026 final rule exempts U.S.-created companies from BOI reporting.
- Copyright transfers/exclusive licenses must be in a signed writing under 17 U.S.C. § 204; Copyright Office recordation is available and can have statutory benefits.
- FTC advertising claims must be truthful, non-deceptive, and evidence-based; a July 2026 FTC publishing-sector action reinforces the risk of earnings/success claims in self-publishing services.
