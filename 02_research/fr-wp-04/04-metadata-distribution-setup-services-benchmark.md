# FR-WP-04 — Metadata, Distribution & Setup Services Benchmark

**Retrieval date:** 2026-09-15

## 1. Service categories

Lane C metadata/distribution assistance can include, when explicitly scoped:
- metadata intake and normalization;
- title/subtitle/contributor validation;
- description/keyword/category preparation;
- BISAC/category support;
- price, publication-date and territorial fields;
- KDP/Ingram/other account setup assistance;
- print and ebook file upload;
- proof-order / proof-approval workflow support;
- preorder configuration;
- metadata correction/update support;
- distribution-status troubleshooting.

These are setup/administrative services. They do not guarantee bookstore stocking, library acquisition, retailer acceptance, discoverability, ranking or sales.

## 2. Gatekeeper Press benchmark

Current public Gatekeeper materials provide several useful scope/change examples:
- metadata optimization is included in its Signature package;
- authors determine their list price and provide/approve metadata;
- preorder setup is publicly priced as an add-on;
- imprint setup is publicly priced as a standalone add-on;
- one metadata-change batch per year is described as included, with later changes carrying a fee;
- post-publication KDP transfer is separately priced;
- an author can use their own imprint; otherwise Gatekeeper may appear as publisher in certain metadata contexts.

These examples show that initial setup, ongoing maintenance, account migration and publisher-identity treatment should be separate scope items.

## 3. BookBaby benchmark

BookBaby publicly markets book distribution and metadata services as part of its full-service self-publishing model, including the ability to make and redeliver metadata changes. Its public positioning emphasizes author creative/financial control and a broad distribution service rather than a guaranteed retail-placement outcome.

Classification: **public provider scope/marketing claim**.

## 4. Ebook Launch boundary example

Ebook Launch's print-formatting service creates files for customer use on supported publishing platforms but does **not** include an ISBN. A customer-provided ISBN can be inserted into the file.

This is a useful specialist-service boundary: producing platform-ready files does not require the service provider to own the identifier or become publisher of record.

## 5. Ekewaka Lane C control posture

The accepted FR-WP-05 architecture remains preferable:
- customer-controlled publishing identity;
- customer-controlled ISBN when an ISBN is required;
- customer-controlled KDP/Ingram/retailer accounts where practicable;
- Ekewaka performs setup under bounded authorization;
- credentials/access should be least-privilege, removable and documented;
- metadata changes require customer approval where they affect commercial/publication identity;
- title revenue should flow directly to the customer where practicable.

## 6. FR-WP-06 labor variables

Model separately:
- initial metadata intake hours;
- metadata QA/reconciliation hours;
- number of formats/ISBNs;
- number of distribution platforms;
- upload/setup time per platform;
- proof/rejection/resubmission cycles;
- preorder setup time;
- post-launch metadata-maintenance allowance;
- account-access/offboarding time;
- customer-support/troubleshooting time;
- channel migration or account-transfer work;
- vendor/platform fees, if any.

## 7. Material risks

- treating "distribution" as a placement promise;
- provider-owned accounts or ISBNs creating lock-in;
- metadata divergence between customer, identifier agency and channels;
- unpriced repeated metadata changes;
- customer delays or incomplete data;
- platform policy changes;
- credential/security exposure;
- accidental publisher-of-record ambiguity.

No final Ekewaka distribution/setup package or maintenance policy is approved by this benchmark.
