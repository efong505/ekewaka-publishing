# FR-WP-04 — Package Pricing, Scope & Revision Benchmark

**Retrieval date:** 2026-09-15

## 1. Representative public package structures

### Gatekeeper Press

Gatekeeper's current Signature package is displayed from approximately **$3,500** and bundles cover design, paperback/eBook formatting and distribution, ISBN/barcode support, metadata optimization, proof/copies, preorder/imprint setup and project management. Editorial services can be configured separately.

Gatekeeper also publishes numerous add-on and change-order prices, making it useful for scope-control benchmarking even though Ekewaka should not copy its price structure.

### BookBaby

BookBaby's current public cost guidance describes:
- typical full-service bundles around **$1,500–$3,000**;
- a professionally fully produced book around **$2,000–$7,000**, depending on editing, custom cover, formatting, print options and quantity;
- cover design around **$399–$599** in its public guidance;
- interior formatting around **$100–$399** in its public guidance.

These are provider marketing/pricing statements, not independently audited industry averages.

### Destiny House Publishing

Current homepage package prices observed:
- Blue: **$799**;
- Silver: **$1,099**;
- Platinum: **$1,399**;
- Diamond: **$1,900**.

However, current individual product pages display materially lower amounts for Silver, Platinum and Diamond: approximately **$450 / $600 / $800**. Product pages also show a 50% deposit option or payment in full.

Because the same provider displays conflicting current public prices, Destiny House package prices are **not sufficiently reliable to anchor Ekewaka pricing**. Its package progression remains useful as an inclusion/scope comparator.

## 2. Payment-pattern observations

Observed public patterns include:
- percentage deposit plus balance/full-payment option (Destiny House);
- recurring monthly installment collection until paid (Gatekeeper terms);
- payment before specialist production begins (common specialist-vendor pattern).

These are market observations only. Ekewaka deposit percentage, installment count, cancellation/refund treatment and milestone timing remain Human Owner decision variables subject to financial and legal review.

## 3. Revision/change-order observations

The strongest benchmark pattern is **limited or conditional included revisions plus paid changes after approval**.

Examples:
- Gatekeeper publishes hourly or tiered charges for additional edits/reformatting/content changes.
- Ebook Launch distinguishes active-project revisions from post-approval/post-final changes and can reprice large revision batches as a reformat.
- specialist providers commonly distinguish correction of their own production error from customer-requested content or direction changes.

## 4. Required package variables

Before any Ekewaka package price can be responsibly modeled, define:
- manuscript word ceiling;
- page ceiling or complexity band;
- exact editorial service and pass count;
- cover concept count and art allowance;
- print/ebook/hardcover format count;
- platform/setup scope;
- ISBN/publisher-identity boundary;
- included author copies and shipping treatment;
- website page/function count, if included;
- marketing asset count;
- consultation/project-management allowance;
- included revision rounds;
- author response deadlines;
- change-order triggers;
- rush policy;
- refund/cancellation milestone logic;
- third-party cost/pass-through rules;
- post-publication support period;
- file/source-asset handoff.

## 5. Package versus à-la-carte architecture

### Packages
Advantages:
- simpler customer decision;
- coordinated workflow;
- predictable baseline deliverables;
- potential operational standardization.

Risks:
- hidden cross-subsidy between heavy/light manuscripts;
- bundled features customers may not need;
- scope ambiguity;
- revision burden;
- marketing-expectation inflation.

### À-la-carte
Advantages:
- clearer service-to-price traceability;
- better fit to manuscript readiness;
- easier change-order accounting.

Risks:
- fragmented customer experience;
- more quoting/PM overhead;
- underestimating dependencies between services.

A later Ekewaka architecture can use packages as **bounded combinations of separately costed service modules** rather than as opaque bundles.

## 6. Pricing rule for later modeling — not approval

Competitor price is context, not cost. For each module/package, later pricing should begin with:

`fully loaded delivery cost = labor + contractor/vendor + software/assets + PM/QA + copies/shipping + payment/admin + expected refund/chargeback loss`

FR-WP-06 can then apply a Human Owner-approved margin variable and validated processor/tax inputs. FR-WP-04 does not set that margin variable.
