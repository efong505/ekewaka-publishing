## 1. Governance and scope

FR-WP-04 is bounded Phase 1 research for **Lane C — Ekewaka Author Services**. It refines the already PM-accepted-with-conditions FR-WP-06 foundational Lane C cost architecture but does not convert that model into a final commercial price list.

The governing three-lane separation remains:

- **Lane A — trade publishing:** selectively acquired, publisher-funded, separate rights/ISBN/accounting architecture.
- **Lane B — hybrid/co-investment:** research only; no launch authority.
- **Lane C — fee-for-service author services:** customer pays for defined deliverables and should normally remain their own publisher, retain rights, control publisher identity/ISBNs/accounts, and receive book-sale revenue directly where practicable.

This package does **not** approve final Ekewaka service prices, packages, deposits, refunds, revisions, margin floors, client intake, contracts, vendor commitments, ISBN purchases, publisher-of-record treatment, paid marketing, or Lane C launch.

## 3. Executive findings

### 3.1 Competitor structure

The market spans at least four different Lane C archetypes:

- **Marketplace procurement:** Reedsy lets authors contract directly with vetted freelancers; prices vary by professional and scope.
- **Full-service self-publishing providers:** BookBaby and Gatekeeper Press bundle file creation, formatting, distribution setup, metadata, ISBN-related support, project management, and optional marketing.
- **Lower-price package providers:** Destiny House Publishing publishes highly bundled packages with editing, design, formatting, ISBN/barcode, marketing collateral, websites, and copies; however, its homepage and product pages currently display materially inconsistent prices.
- **Specialist production vendors:** Ebook Launch focuses on design/formatting with unusually explicit revision/change-order mechanics.

This heterogeneity means competitor sticker prices are not directly comparable without normalizing editorial depth, word/page ceilings, file formats, revision rounds, account/ISBN treatment, author copies, marketing assets, and project-management intensity.

### 3.2 Editing economics are the largest hidden package-cost risk

Current 2026 Reedsy marketplace data places editing broadly around 1.4¢–5.3¢ per word depending on service. For an 80,000-word book, Reedsy reports roughly $1,920–$4,560 for one professional editorial engagement, with developmental editing higher than copyediting/proofreading.

The 2026 Editorial Freelancers Association rate chart independently reinforces the labor intensity. For nonfiction:
- copyediting: 3.0¢–4.0¢/word, $45–$60/hour, roughly 5–9 pages/hour;
- developmental editing: 4.0¢–5.0¢/word, $57.50–$70/hour, roughly 4–8 pages/hour;
- formatting: about 2.0¢/word, $60–$70/hour, roughly 7–15 pages/hour.

A package promising broadly defined “editing” at a low flat price without a word ceiling, editorial level, manuscript-condition qualifier, number of passes, and change-order rule is structurally exposed to underpricing.

### 3.3 Design / production benchmarks support explicit complexity bands

Reedsy’s 2026 cover-design analysis reports an average professional cover project of about $880, with most projects around $625–$1,250. Complexity, illustration, multiple formats, series branding, revision rounds, and rush timing raise cost.

EFA’s 2026 chart shows book-cover graphic-design work around $50/hour and a median reported project price around $350, while book/print layout is around $50–$55/hour. These association figures are survey medians, not quality-normalized package prices.

Specialist vendor Ebook Launch demonstrates a strong operational pattern: quote-based formatting, a defined supported-platform scope, one free formatting-related correction set, then per-revision/minimum fees, and reformat pricing when revisions exceed a threshold.

### 3.4 Package prices cannot be copied across providers

Representative current public package structures include:
- Gatekeeper Press Signature package: currently displayed from about $3,500, with package-level design, paperback/eBook formatting and distribution, ISBN/barcode, metadata optimization, proof/copies, preorder/imprint setup, and project management; editing can be added separately.
- BookBaby: public guidance places full-service bundles around $1,500–$3,000 and a professionally fully produced book around $2,000–$7,000, depending heavily on editing, design, formatting, print quantity and options.
- Destiny House Publishing: homepage currently advertises Blue $799, Silver $1,099, Platinum $1,399 and Diamond $1,900, while individual product pages currently show materially lower Silver/Platinum/Diamond amounts ($450/$600/$800). This inconsistency is a material reliability warning; Destiny House prices should not be used as a durable Ekewaka pricing anchor without live reconfirmation.

The package architecture is still useful: higher tiers tend to add websites, trailers, event collateral, launch assets, additional copies, and broader editorial allowance.

### 3.5 Revision/change-order design is a first-order margin control

Gatekeeper and Ebook Launch provide the clearest public examples:
- Gatekeeper prices additional edits/reformatting at $125/hour in several contexts and publishes tiered content-change fees.
- Ebook Launch generally permits active-project revisions to the approved concept, but post-approval changes are quoted; formatting corrections have minimum fees and 30+ revisions can trigger full reformat pricing.

Ekewaka should treat revision rounds, author-supplied post-approval content changes, new creative direction, changed trim/page count, and post-publication changes as separate variables.

### 3.6 Project-management burden is economically material

Gatekeeper explicitly assigns an Author Manager through 30 days after publication. EFA’s 2026 rate survey reports project-management work at roughly $65–$75/hour with a median project value around $1,250. This does not mean Ekewaka should charge those rates; it demonstrates that coordination time is a real professional service cost.

Lane C modeling should therefore include intake, scheduling, file/version control, vendor coordination, author communications, QA, metadata handoff, proof approval, and project closeout rather than treating them as free overhead.

### 3.7 Websites, marketing, publicity and coaching require narrow deliverables

Current market evidence is highly variable:
- Reedsy reports 2025 marketplace author-website projects averaging about $1,640; fewer than 10% were under $500 and more than 10% exceeded $2,500.
- Reedsy reports a median of about $1,370 for a month-long book-publicity campaign.
- EFA reports coaching around $75–$100/hour and project management around $65–$75/hour.
- Destiny House publishes a writing-coach offer starting at $75/week for a tightly constrained 500-word weekly review plus accountability communications.

These services should be sold, if ever approved, as bounded work products or time allowances, never as sales/review/bestseller/bookstore-placement guarantees.

## 4. Lane C rights/account/ISBN conclusion

The accepted FR-WP-05 architecture remains the safer Ekewaka default:

- customer remains publisher of record;
- customer owns or directly controls their ISBN/publishing identity;
- customer controls KDP/Ingram/other retailer/distributor accounts where practicable;
- Ekewaka uses narrow, purpose-limited service permissions;
- customer receives agreed final/source files;
- provider access is removable at closeout;
- customer revenue flows directly to customer where practicable.

Current competitors illustrate why these controls matter. Gatekeeper states that authors retain 100% of rights, ISBNs it assigns, created files after full payment, and royalties, but also states that Gatekeeper may be listed as publisher unless the customer uses its imprint setup. Destiny House says authors retain publishing rights yet bundles an ISBN/barcode. Therefore, rights retention alone does not resolve publisher-of-record or ISBN-control ambiguity.

Ekewaka should not default to supplying an Ekewaka-owned ISBN to Lane C clients and should not make Ekewaka publisher of record merely because it performed production services.

Highest-priority Lane C risks are:

- `RISK-002` trade/services/hybrid confusion;

- `RISK-009` misleading author-service marketing;
- `RISK-014` excessive operational complexity;

FR-WP-04 additionally identifies:
- revision and post-approval scope creep;
- editorial-level ambiguity;
- public-price volatility/inconsistency;
- provider/account/ISBN/publisher-of-record ambiguity;
- bundled marketing expectations;
- customer-supplied content/permissions risk;
- refund/chargeback and installment-collection exposure;
- capacity compression when multiple editing-heavy projects overlap.
