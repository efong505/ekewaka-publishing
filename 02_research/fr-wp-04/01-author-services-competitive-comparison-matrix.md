## Cross-comparator conclusions

1. **No single sticker price is comparable without normalized scope.** A $600 package can include superficially similar labels to a $3,500 package while differing materially in editorial depth, project-management time, revision rights, file ownership, distribution support and post-publication obligations.
2. **Professional editing alone can exceed many low-price package stickers.** This is the clearest warning against copying inexpensive bundles.
3. **Project management is a real deliverable.** Gatekeeper's Author Manager structure and EFA's PM rate data support explicit PM-hour costing.
4. **Customer-control claims must be decomposed.** Rights, ISBN ownership, publisher-of-record metadata, retailer accounts, source files and revenue routing are separate control dimensions.
5. **Marketing support is not an outcome guarantee.** A provider can deliver graphics, publicity work, metadata, ads setup or launch consultation without guaranteeing sales, reviews or retail placement.

## Ekewaka benchmark posture

Use competitors to define scope choices and customer expectations. Use bottom-up labor/vendor costing to price. Do not set Ekewaka prices by averaging competitor packages.
