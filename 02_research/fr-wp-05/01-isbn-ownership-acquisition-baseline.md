# FR-WP-05 — ISBN Ownership & Acquisition Baseline

## Authoritative U.S. baseline
- Bowker / the U.S. ISBN Agency is the authorized ISBN agency for publishers with U.S. addresses.
- The agency record ties an assigned publisher prefix/ISBN block to one publisher of record.
- Publisher-assigned ISBNs are not properly resold, split, transferred or reassigned to another publisher.
- ISBNs identify publication products; they do **not** establish copyright ownership.
- Each distinct format/binding normally needs its own ISBN; revised editions need new ISBNs; assigned ISBNs are never reused.

## Publisher-owned vs platform-provided
**Publisher-controlled ISBN:** supports the publisher's own imprint/publisher identity and, subject to platform rules, can be used for the same edition across multiple service/distribution platforms.

**Platform-provided ISBN:** may be owned/controlled by the platform and may lock the bibliographic publisher/imprint to that platform's identity. Current examples researched: KDP `Independently published`, IngramSpark `Indy Pub`, Lulu/Lulu.com under the free-ISBN agreement.

## Ekewaka planning implication
For Lane A editions, a publisher-controlled ISBN architecture is the preferred research recommendation because Ekewaka intends to operate as a selective publisher across multiple channels. This is **not** approval to buy ISBNs or assign them.

## Acquisition planning assumptions for FR-WP-06
- Model blocks/quantities rather than assuming one ISBN per title.
- Estimate consumption by format and edition: paperback + hardcover + separately identified ebook + audiobook can consume multiple identifiers for one title.
- Include reserve capacity for revised editions, format expansion, publisher migration and corrected/relaunched products.
- Use live Bowker pricing at the time a purchase decision is presented; do not hard-code stale pricing from secondary sources.
