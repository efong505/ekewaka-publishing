# FR-WP-05 — Foundational Publishing Rights Framework

## Legal/operational distinction
This document identifies contract and registry categories for later professional review. It does not state what Ekewaka is legally entitled to acquire in any specific transaction and does not provide legal advice.

## Copyright ownership baseline
U.S. copyright initially vests in the author(s) unless an applicable doctrine such as work made for hire changes that result. Copyright ownership is distinct from ownership of copies and from ISBN ownership. Copyright can be transferred in whole or in part.

An exclusive license of one or more exclusive copyright rights is treated as a transfer of copyright ownership under 17 U.S.C. §101; a nonexclusive license is not. Transfers of copyright ownership are generally subject to the writing/signature requirement in 17 U.S.C. §204. Statutory termination rights may apply to certain grants; counsel review is required before drafting reversion/termination language.

## Rights-registry fields
A future rights registry should capture:
- work/title and edition scope;
- copyright owner(s);
- contract counterparty;
- rights granted;
- rights retained;
- exclusive / nonexclusive status;
- language;
- territory;
- print rights;
- ebook/digital rights;
- audiobook/audio rights;
- translation rights;
- adaptation/derivative rights if addressed;
- serialization/excerpt rights if addressed;
- subsidiary-rights categories;
- sublicensing authority;
- term/start/end dates;
- renewal/extension logic;
- reversion/termination triggers;
- publication/availability obligations if adopted;
- royalty/accounting basis reference;
- audit/statement rights;
- permissions/warranties/indemnity review flags;
- AI-generated/assisted content, artwork, training and voice rights flags;
- contract status and counsel-review status.

## Rights granted vs retained
The contract and registry should positively identify both what is granted and what remains with the author. Silence should not be treated operationally as permission to exploit a format, territory, language or subsidiary right.

## Exclusive vs nonexclusive
Exclusivity can materially affect author freedom, publisher investment, sublicense strategy, termination/reversion exposure and accounting. RIGHTS-01 does not recommend a universal exclusivity rule; the scope must be justified by the business model and reviewed by publishing/IP counsel.

## Permissions
Third-party text, images, artwork, translations, quotations, fonts, cover assets and other licensed material may create separate permission limits even if Ekewaka holds publishing rights from the author. Production should not infer permissions from the author-publisher agreement alone.
