# FR-WP-03 — Short-Run, Offset, and Local Printing Baseline

**Retrieval date:** 2026-09-10

## 1. Short-run digital printing
Short-run digital printing is the principal bridge between POD and offset. It is most useful when Ekewaka has a known event/bulk requirement, needs better unit economics or production options than POD, but cannot justify a large inventory commitment.

### 48 Hour Books
Current public evidence:
- minimum book order: **10 copies**;
- no stated maximum; large orders can be produced in stages;
- per-book pricing generally falls as quantity increases;
- orders of **100+** currently receive **25 additional books free**;
- current 2026 materials state additional price breaks for **500, 1,000, 2,500, and 5,000** books;
- published example: order 100 copies of a 6×9, 180-page, black-and-white perfect-bound paperback and receive 125 total; effective manufacturing price is stated as **$4.74/book** after the 25 extra copies;
- shipping and project customization must still be separately considered.

This is useful benchmark evidence, not an Ekewaka quote.

### Bookmobile
Current public evidence:
- publisher-oriented short-run digital printer;
- minimum run **25 copies**;
- public examples include short-run paperback and hardcover production;
- supports perfect binding, hardcover, spiral/wire-O and specialty stocks/finishes;
- fulfillment services can store inventory, accept drop-ship orders, upload lists, and support API feeds;
- printing is invoiced when a run enters inventory and fulfillment costs are invoiced weekly according to its public fulfillment page.

Bookmobile is a strong quote candidate where Ekewaka needs publisher-style short-run production plus fulfillment, but current title-specific pricing requires a quote.

## 2. Offset printing
Offset printing is economically attractive when fixed setup/prepress costs can be spread over enough units, but it shifts risk from per-order manufacturing to publisher-owned inventory.

### PrintNinja benchmark
Current public evidence for relevant book products:
- minimum **250 copies** for hardcover books and art books;
- vendor states offset becomes lower per unit than POD/digital at these higher quantities;
- current hardcover page cites **4–6 weeks production** after file approval plus approximately **6–8 weeks ocean shipping** or about a week by air;
- specialty materials/finishing and freight materially affect final cost.

This supports using 250–500 copies as the first serious offset comparison range, not as an automatic breakpoint.

## 3. Local / regional New Mexico candidates
No local vendor below had enough public, book-specific pricing to establish apples-to-apples unit economics. They are **vendor-validation candidates**, not approved vendors.

### Aiken Printing Co. — Albuquerque
Publicly lists:
- traditional offset printing;
- full-color short-run digital printing;
- perfect binding and other bindery;
- inventory and fulfillment;
- mailing services.

This is the strongest public local capability match found for an integrated short-run/fulfillment quote.

### Starline Printing — Albuquerque
Publicly states it operates:
- heat-set web press;
- Heidelberg sheet-fed presses;
- digital presses;
- custom quote workflow.

Book-specific binding, unit pricing and fulfillment must be confirmed directly through a formal quote.

### The Printer's Press — Albuquerque
Public site lists booklet production and states color copies are suitable for short runs of **500 or fewer**. Current book-like jobs require quote/specification review.

### Business Printing Services — Albuquerque
Public site has a dedicated books/booklets service page. Detailed specifications/pricing are not public enough for modeling.

### Graphic Sky — Santa Fe
Publicly offers perfect-bound books and other bound-document printing with a real-time/custom quote workflow. Quote excludes tax/shipping unless specified.

### Paper Tiger — Santa Fe
Publicly lists books/manuals under commercial print and direct/bulk mail services. Book-specific print and fulfillment pricing requires quote.

### Ortiz Printing — Santa Fe
Publicly identifies commercial and offset printing plus hard- and soft-cover binding. Quantity-specific economics require validation.

## 4. POD vs short-run vs offset tradeoff
| Dimension | POD | Short-run digital | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cash | Lowest | Medium | Highest |
| Unsold inventory | Minimal | Moderate | Highest |
| Unit cost at large volume | Highest | Medium | Often lowest |
| Speed for 1 copy | Strong | Weak/medium | Weak |
| Event stock | Possible but landed freight may hurt | Strong | Strong if planned far ahead |
| Specialty options | Limited by platform | Broader | Broadest |
| Warehouse requirement | None | Possible | Usually yes |
| Reprint flexibility | Excellent | Good | Lower / batch-oriented |
| Demand-risk tolerance | Best for uncertain demand | Best for bounded known demand | Best only when demand is validated |

## 5. Quote protocol for later vendor validation
Every printer quote should use the same specification sheet:
- trim size;
- page count;
- B&W/color and paper stock;
- binding;
- cover stock/finish;
- quantity points: 25/50/100/250/500/1,000/5,000 where supported;
- proofs/prepress/setup/revision charges;
- packaging/cartons/pallets;
- freight to Albuquerque and any alternate warehouse;
- production lead time;
- damage/reprint policy;
- storage/fulfillment terms;
- payment timing;
- file retention/reorder terms;
- domestic vs offshore production location.

## 6. Recommended modeling posture
- 1–25: POD dominates unless specialty production is required.
- 25–100: compare digital short-run for events/direct stock.
- 100–250: short-run digital becomes a meaningful economic alternative.
- 250–500: require digital and offset comparisons.
- 500–1,000: offset often deserves stronger weighting, but only on landed cost and sell-through.
- 5,000+: treat as procurement/inventory strategy with explicit Human Owner approval.
